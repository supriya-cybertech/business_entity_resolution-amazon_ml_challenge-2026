## 1. Executive Summary
Our solution implements a highly scalable, two-stage Entity Resolution pipeline designed to reconcile noisy business records across disparate sources. We utilized a composite heuristic blocking strategy to drastically reduce the O(N²) search space, followed by a LightGBM classifier with F_0.5 calibrated thresholding to maximize precision and eliminate false merges.

---

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Problem Analysis
During exploratory data analysis (EDA), we established that the datasets lacked shared identifiers and contained severe noise, including missing PIN codes, legal suffix variations (e.g., Pvt. vs. Private), transliteration inconsistencies, and landmark-based address formats. A pure cross-join comparison was computationally prohibitive, and standard exact-matching failed due to heavy string variations.

### 2.2 Solution Strategy
To handle these challenges under strict constraints, we opted for an optimized, feature-rich tree-based pipeline over latency-heavy deep learning architectures.

**Approach Type:** Blocking + Classifier Pipeline  
**Core Innovation:** The implementation of a high-recall "Cheap Key" blocking step combined with RapidFuzz-based advanced string similarity features. We heavily penalized false merges by calibrating our classification boundary to a strict probability threshold (>0.88), specifically tailored to optimize the Macro F_0.5 metric.

---

## 3. Candidate Generation (Blocking)
Comparing every Source 1 record against all Source 2/3 records results in excessive computational overhead. We engineered a low-cost, deterministic grouping mechanism to scale candidate generation.

- **Blocking keys used:** A composite `cheap_key` generated by concatenating the first 4 alphanumeric characters of the standardized `business_name` and the first 3 characters of the standardized `business_address`.
- **Candidate pairs generated:** This approach reduced millions of potential combinations down to a highly targeted, manageable subset of plausible candidate pairs per Source 1 entity.
- **How you ensured true matches were not lost:** By heavily cleaning and standardizing the text (lowercasing and stripping non-alphanumeric characters) prior to key extraction, we ensured that typos and minor variations at the end of the strings did not affect the initial grouping.

---

## 4. Matching Model

**Features used:**
- **Name features:** RapidFuzz Token Set Ratio, Token Sort Ratio, Jaro-Winkler normalized similarity, and absolute length differences.
- **Address features:** RapidFuzz Token Set Ratio, Jaro-Winkler normalized similarity, and absolute length differences.
- **Other:** Implicit region-specific pattern matching handled by the gradient boosting splits based on substring distances.

**Model type:** LightGBM Classifier (`n_estimators=500`, `class_weight='balanced'`).  
**Threshold selection method:** F_0.5 Calibration. Because the Macro F_0.5 metric weights precision twice as heavily as recall, we shifted our decision boundary to a strict threshold (probability > 0.88). If the model's confidence fell below this threshold, we opted not to merge, prioritizing exact matches and safely capturing full scores on Singleton entities.
